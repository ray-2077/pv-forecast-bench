\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Holding the model, the data and the training procedure fixed, we
varied only the reference forecast and the treatment of night hours.
Baseline selection does not merely rescale reported skill; it can
reverse the qualitative conclusion about how forecast advantage varies
with horizon. The reference forecast changes both the magnitude and
the shape of reported skill: on the held-out test year, 67 percent of
the apparent skill relative to persistence disappears when the same
forecasts are evaluated against the convex reference at a six-hour
horizon (70 percent on the validation split, an upper bound, since the
reference's weight is fitted there), and the trend inverts from
monotonically increasing to a peak at three hours - a shape inversion
established cleanly on the held-out test year, where the convex
reference's weight is never fit, and supported by Holm-adjusted
Diebold-Mariano tests on that same test split, with the corresponding
validation comparisons reported descriptively. Including night hours
deflates normalised error by approximately 34 percent, closed-form in
the daylight fraction, while leaving skill nearly unchanged. Both
effects exceed the largest architectural effect we measure on the test
split, 0.086 in skill score, including the residual correction stage;
restricted to the three base architectures alone the test-split spread
is smaller still, 0.028, making the reference-forecast effect
approximately 16.6 times larger in that narrower comparison and 5.46
times larger overall.

\subsection{Recommendations}
\label{sec:conclusion-recommendations}

Three practices, none of them novel, would make results in this
literature comparable. Report a skill score against a stated reference
forecast: against smart persistence alone our results appear to
improve steadily with horizon, against the convex combination of
climatology and persistence recommended by Yang et
al.~\cite{yang2020verification} they do not, and the two references
support opposite conclusions from identical forecasts. Report the
evaluation sample: whether night hours are included, what daylight
threshold is applied, and how many samples each cell contains are each
sufficient to move a reported error by more than the improvement
typically claimed. Report run-to-run variance: none of the 27 papers
we surveyed does, and a single run cannot separate a genuine effect
from retraining noise.

supporting evidence are available at an anonymised repository, link
