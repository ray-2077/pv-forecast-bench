\begin{abstract}
Photovoltaic power forecasting studies report large accuracy gains
under widely varying protocol: of 27 recent papers we coded, 26 report
no skill score against a reference forecast and all report point
estimates without run-to-run variance. Using a leakage-controlled protocol on three
co-located arrays (900 recorded runs, five architectures, five seeds),
we show the reference forecast determines both the magnitude and the
shape of reported skill. Against smart persistence, skill rises
monotonically with horizon, read as a model's advantage growing at
longer lead times; against the optimal convex combination of
climatology and persistence, the same forecasts give non-monotonic
skill peaking at three hours. Night hours deflate normalised error by
approximately 34 percent, closed-form in the daylight fraction, while
skill is almost unchanged. Reported accuracy here is shaped more by
the evaluation protocol than by the model: on the test year, the
reference effect (0.469) is 5.5 times the largest architectural
effect we measure across all five architectures (0.086), and 16.6
times the base-architecture spread alone (0.028), both test-split
figures.
\end{abstract}
