\section{Data and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Site and Arrays}
\label{sec:data-site-arrays}

Measurements are from the Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre
(DKASC) at Alice Springs (23.767 deg S, 133.867 deg E), an open
dataset of ground-mounted PV installations with per-array power and a
co-located weather station \cite{dkasolarcentre}. Three fixed-mount
arrays, chosen for differing module technology at comparable scale,
are used: site 11 (BP Solar, polycrystalline silicon, 5.0 kW), site 12
(BP Solar, monocrystalline silicon, 5.1 kW), and site 17 (Sanyo,
heterojunction, 6.3 kW). These are three co-located arrays, not three
sites: they share one weather station and experience the same sky, so
their forecast errors are correlated and results across them do not
constitute independent replications - consistency across them is
consistency across module technology under common meteorology, not
geographic generalisation.

Data span 2009 to 2015 at hourly resolution and are split
chronologically into whole calendar years: training 2011-2013 (26,304
rows per array, beginning in 2011 because site 17 was not commissioned
until 2010), validation 2014 (8,760 rows), and test 2015 (8,760 rows,
evaluable to 3,802 daylight hours on sites 11 and 12 and 3,757 on site
17 after its documented 5-9 June 2015 outage) - full metadata in the
repository (available at an anonymised repository, link withheld for
review).

\subsection{Excluded Array}
\label{sec:data-excluded-array}

A fourth array, site 7 (First Solar, cadmium telluride, 7.0 kW), was
initially included and later excluded. A conventional completeness
audit passed it for 2014 at 99.99 percent coverage, yet 48.41 percent
of that year's daylight hours record exactly zero output, with a
further 48-day near-zero period in November-December 2015, inside the
test year. The audit did not detect this because the logger continued
to record faithfully, and what it recorded was zero: zero is not
missing, and a completeness check is structurally blind to a healthy
sensor reporting a dead array. A second audit, testing for near-zero
rather than exactly-zero output, is required to catch an analogous
case on site 17 (reported in full in the repository).

\subsection{Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data-preprocessing}

Raw five-minute measurements were localised to Australia/Darwin
(UTC+9:30) and verified empirically (mean hour of maximum measured
irradiance 11.97 against 12.00 modelled, agreeing within one minute).
Implausible values were set to missing, negative readings clipped to
zero, and data resampled to hourly means using hour-beginning labels.
Three recorded channels - cumulative delivered energy, performance
ratio, and average phase current - were discarded as deterministic
functions of measured power and therefore inadmissible as features.

\subsection{Clear-Sky Reference}
\label{sec:data-clearsky}

Clear-sky global horizontal irradiance is computed with the
Ineichen-Perez model \cite{ineichen2002linke, holmgren2018pvlib} at
five-minute resolution and averaged to hourly using the same
convention as the measurements, since an instantaneous midpoint value
differs systematically from an hourly mean near sunrise and sunset;
solar position, by contrast, is evaluated at the hour midpoint, since
it describes the hour rather than being compared against an average.
Clear-sky power is modelled through a physical chain - solar position,
irradiance, transposition, a training-period temperature climatology,
and PVWatts DC conversion - followed by a single scalar gain per
array, fitted on training years only: 0.914, 0.907 and 1.004 for sites
11, 12 and 17, the last exceeding unity for reasons not diagnosed. The
gain is fitted on training data only, so it cancels exactly in the
clear-sky-index formulation and both model families are invariant to
it by construction.
