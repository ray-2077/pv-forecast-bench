**key_claim**: "For day-ahead (24-48h) forecasting of 14 Hungarian PV
plants using AROME NWP input, a hybrid model that feeds physically-
calculated PV power (and other physical intermediates) into an ML model
reduces MAE by 5.2% versus an optimized pure-physical model chain and by
10.4% versus ML with no physical predictors, when trained on 2 years of
data (2019-2020) and tested on the full year 2021; the hybrid advantage
requires at least ~1 year of training data, and below that the paper
recommends staying with optimized physical modeling." | "these hybrid
methods offer a 5.2% MAE and 1.0% RMSE decrease over the physical modeling
and a 10.4% MAE and 3.3% RMSE decrease over the ML that does not involve
any physical considerations" (p.13, Conclusion); "the hybrid method is only
beneficial for at least one year of training data" (Abstract).

## What this paper does WELL (record per instructions - exceptions matter)

- Explicitly excludes night hours with a stated, reproducible rule (3
  numbered criteria, not a vague mention).
- Uses the Yang et al. (2020) optimal convex combination of climatology and
  persistence as its skill-score reference - the most rigorous baseline
  construction seen in this survey so far, matching this project's own
  headline-metric choice.
- Explicit, stated chronological train/test split with named calendar years
  and an explicit statement that the test year was untouched during
  training/selection ("not involved in the training and selection of the
  models in any way").
- Runs the same comparison across three training-window lengths (half-year,
  one year, two years) as a stated sensitivity analysis (Section 3.3) - the
  closest thing to a protocol-sensitivity result found in this batch so far.
  Directly relevant to this project's own Finding 7 (training length).
- States an explicit data-availability position, even though it is a
  negative one ("confidential").

## Other observations / consistency checks

- Numbers reconcile internally: the abstract's "5.2% MAE / 10.4% MAE"
  figures match the Conclusion's restatement exactly (5.2%/1.0% RMSE and
  10.4%/3.3% RMSE) - no discrepancy found.
- No MAPE used (paper explicitly reasons about MAE vs RMSE directive choice
  instead, citing Murphy & Epstein 1989 and the consistency argument -
  another point in its favour on evaluation rigor).
- Did not attempt the RMSE^2 ~ mean^2 + SD^2 reconciliation: the paper
  reports normalized (percentage) errors aggregated per plant in a bar
  figure and per-training-window table, not raw per-timestep residual mean
  and SD on a stated common sample, so there is no single (mean, SD, RMSE)
  triple on the same sample to check against each other from the extracted
  text.
- Shared-author/institution check: single author (Mayer, Budapest
  University of Technology and Economics). No overlap with other papers
  coded so far in this survey.
